# A0 verification and known limitations

This repository is an **engineering starter**, dated 2026-11-08. No physical keyboard, PCB, enclosure, USB isolation circuit or display has been built or tested. The Sculpt-style shell, revised 2026-11-12, is a geometry revision of that starter. It is envelope CAD, not a fabrication release, and its physical fit is unmeasured. The PCBs were routed on 2026-11-14 and come with a **prototype order package** for PCBWay. That package is not a release: no board has been built, and AGENTS.md still requires a person to review the USB pairs, the ground planes and return paths, and every fabrication layer before an order.

The CAD checks are topology and envelope checks. They do not establish switch retention, stabilizer compatibility, connector fit, display glass/PCB height, print shrinkage, fastener strength, rigidity, ergonomics or assembly clearance against detailed vendor models. The PCB shape shown in STEP is an envelope, not a fully populated board model. Keycaps shown in the assembly are solid envelopes without stems.

Known unfinished work is explicit in [roadmap.md](roadmap.md): the human review of both routed boards, USB/power measurements, display retention, a printed check of the center tab, per-key RGB power, and all physical bring-up. This grid has no key wider than 1u, so the generated plates have no stabilizer slots. The plate has a south window at each key. The RGB header is not a stuffed LED chain, and a chain would exceed USB suspend without a power switch this MCU cannot currently enable. The chassis sits flat. The riser sets 6.7 degrees of negative tilt; it is modeled, not built. The rim is flat. The keywells are not roll-tented. The selected host-input arrangement does not include Launch's USB hub.

Case shell, not verified:

- No slicer run and no printed or machined part. Fit of the plate in its recess, the top cover in the rim, the palm rest on its shelf, and the riser under the front is unmeasured.
- The top cover is held by eight M2.5 screws from below into blind 2.1 mm pilots, 6.7 mm deep on MX and 3.9 mm on Choc v1. Thread engagement in printed plastic is untested. The cover has a 1.0 mm web between the display pocket and each key window.
- USB plug reach, magnet fit and pull, pad seating, palm screw engagement, and the riser stance. Pads are taken as 2.0 mm proud; the pad part is not chosen.
- On Choc v1 the three USB mouths are open notches through the rim and the top cover. The rim is too low to bridge a plug. The side mouths cross the shoulder at an angle, so their two walls differ in length.
- The palm rest rear and wing-side edges are square in CAD. Edges cut after the dress pass are sharp. Split joinery is unchanged and unvalidated; the riser is one 224 mm piece.
- The interference check uses envelopes. Keycaps are boxes without stems or wobble. Switch housings use nominal sizes.
- The outline is tangent arcs, not curvature-continuous. Curvature steps 7.9 times at the lip-to-corner joint.
- The 4.5 mm sweep under the wall is a plain 45° chamfer, not a convex section.
- `assets/layout.svg` and `assets/layout.png` still show the earlier short palm rest, not the shell. The product page and `assets/product.png` use path-traced renders from `tools/render.py`. The case parts in them come from the CAD model. The keycaps, legends, switch housings, plain PCB, display content and colours are drawn for the picture in `tools/render_parts.py` and are not design data. The renders are not photographs and are not evidence of fit. The `palm` and `palm_screws` keys in `assets/layout.json` describe that earlier rest. See the authority exception in [design.md](design.md).

PCB and case, as of the routed boards:

- **USB jacks.** J1, J2 and J3 sit on F.Cu, turned so each mouth is flush with the board's rear edge at the case mouth: `layout.mouths()` and `cad/model.py` `_mouths()` agree at x = −64, 0 and 64 mm, y = −59.6 mm, and the receptacle body (7.3 mm, HRO drawing) ends at the edge. HRO's drawing shows the shell 0.4 mm proud of the PCB edge; this board keeps it flush, as the case model assumes. Check a real receptacle and plug in a printed tunnel.
- **Component sides and heights.** Every SMD part is on B.Cu, in the 4.0 mm between the PCB and the chassis floor. Tallest: hot-swap sockets 3.05 mm (0.95 mm left), SOIC-8 flash 2.16 mm, TS-1088 switches 2.0 mm, SOT-23 parts about 1.45 mm, SOD-123 diodes about 1.35 mm. Bottom parts are seated outside a 4.0 mm radius around each hole (chassis boss R3.6 plus 0.4); hot-swap sockets and diodes follow the keys and were not re-seated. On F.Cu only the receptacles (3.26 mm, inside the plate's port notches) and the through-hole positions J4–J7 remain. A standard 1x3 header is 8.5 mm tall above the board and the J4 socket footprint 5.0 mm (the nearest real part found is 5.7 mm); the room under the plate is 1.0 mm on Choc v1 and 3.5 mm on MX, and the LCD module sits 1.2 mm (Choc v1) or 4.0 mm (MX) above the board. None of J4–J7 can take a standard part; see [assembly.md](assembly.md). The closed case gives no access to BOOT, RESET or J6/J7. Component keep-outs are still not in the CAD fit gate.
- **Mounting holes.** All eight hole centres sit less than a hole radius plus 1.0 mm inside the board edge, so `layout.board_outline()` opens each into a slot through the edge. The chassis and plate holes are unchanged. Whether an open slot holds an M2.5 screw head well enough is unchecked.
- **RP2040 decoupling.** The capacitors stand about 5 mm from their pins so the 30 GPIO pins can fan out between them; each pin and each capacitor reaches the In2.Cu 3V3 area and the ground planes through its own via. That is a longer loop than the RP2040 design guide's layout and needs a review.

Resolved in this revision: the exported solids were the mirror image of the PCB top view. They are now right-handed, and tests hold the eight holes to the PCB positions.

`pcb/fab/` holds a PCBWay prototype order package for each variant: Gerbers, drills, IPC-D-356 netlist, BOM, centroid, assembly drawings, renders and `ORDER.md`. `docs/bom.csv` marks each line `MPN_READ_2026-11-13_STOCK_AND_PRICE_UNKNOWN` when its part number was read on a maker or distributor page, or `UNVERIFIED_NOT_FOR_ORDER`. The shared UF2 is 418304 bytes and was compiled for this 48-key grid. `firmware/artifacts/build-report.json` records `hardware_tested: false`.

`make check-fabrication` must fail at A0, and does: the design-file checks pass, and it names what nothing in the repository can supply: a person's review of the USB pairs, planes and return paths and of every Gerber layer, hardware prototype evidence, the J6/J7 ROM override on hardware, and the fit of an assembled board in the printed case. A passing CI job means the design files are generated and agree; it does not mean a PCB should be ordered unreviewed.

After the initial GitHub push, inspect the workflow run for each new revision; local checks and remote CI are separate evidence. Do not infer remote CI success from this static file.
